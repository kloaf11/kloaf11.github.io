## Reflection on Education

I learned a lot from my time in the Computer Science program at SNHU. I was introduced to programming in several languages, including C++, Python, Java, and R. I learned that rigorous testing and attention to detail are very important when delivering a finished, quality product. The program taught me coding best practices and how to work in an agile environment. Proper testing matters because it reduces the number of bugs that can be missed during coding, including logical bugs. It also tests each module and the system to make sure each part functions as it should. I also learned valuable lessons about cooperation, working with teammates in source control such as Bitbucket, and the importance of maintenance and procedure when using source control. Source control matters because it lets developers work on their own enhancements and test them in isolation before merging them into test branches. It also lets you pull a fresh copy, or go back in a program's history, when a change causes issues or bugs.

### Classes Taken

In IT-145 I learned about app development and how to program for several different kinds of input. I developed a simple but effective zoo monitoring application. This is the artifact I use to showcase my enhancements, and I cover it in the refinement plan.

In CS-250 I learned how the software development lifecycle works and about the many roles in it. I also looked at each role to understand the mindset of the product owner, project manager, testers, and coders, so I could communicate more effectively with teammates.

In CS-310 I learned an important lesson about teamwork and collaboration, and it was my first time using Bitbucket inside an IDE. In this program, my classmates and I contributed side by side to a jukebox program. We added unit tests for each module we added, and we edited the main program with source control so our new methods, data, and other pieces were included.

In CS-340 I learned how client/server development works. We built several server and client programs. We also learned about creating and managing threads on a server and how they interact with a client application, which strengthened our understanding of both sides of the process, including the security those relationships need inside a company.

These are not the only things I learned in the Computer Science program, but they are highlights of the learning and professional development that came with earning my degree. They are wide-ranging lessons I can use with any team I am on in the future, so we can keep learning from each other.

## To the Future

Currently my Senior DBA position is with the state, which is a good learning position in an advanced area of databases, specifically SQL Server. I hope to learn from this experience and either move into a managerial position or a more full-stack position at a private company, preferably with remote working options. My time in the military gave me an aptitude and the skills for leadership. I was the highest individual in my field, which was radio repair. As the only person who knew radio, I also took care of routine maintenance, installation, and inventory management for the radios. I had to make reports and decisions for the entire company regarding the radios, bring them to my company command, and take their feedback to form a plan of action. That plan covered how to achieve our goals, find people, and train them to do the tasks quickly, while making sure they knew exactly what needed to be done. The work then only needed to be checked later, not fixed when I made my rounds. The military taught me attention to detail, how to stick to a schedule, and how to work under pressure. I hope to turn these skills into professional experience in the civilian sector.
