## Introduction

This ePortfolio was prepared for CS-499 in February 2021. Phrases such as "currently" and "after I get my degree" describe that time.

I am Kenneth Borges. I currently work as a Senior Database Analyst, and I have been working on my Computer Science B.S. degree for a few years. I am a veteran of the Army. After I get my degree, I would look for a position where I can improve my existing skills as a SQL developer and administrator. This portfolio enhances one project in several ways, with a plan for each enhancement.

## Reflection on Education

I learned a lot from my time in the Computer Science program at SNHU. I have been introduced to programming in quite a few languages, including C++, Python, Java, and R. I have learned that rigorous testing of programs and attention to detail are very important if you want to deliver a finished, quality product. The program taught me best practices for coding as well as how to work in an agile environment. Proper testing is important because it lets you reduce the number of bugs that may have been missed during coding, including logical bugs. It also tests each module and the system to make sure each part functions as it should. In addition, I learned valuable lessons about cooperation and about working with teammates in source control, such as Bitbucket, and about the importance of maintenance and procedure when using source control. Source control is important because it lets developers work on their own enhancements and test them in isolation before merging them into test branches with any other changes. It also lets you pull a fresh copy, or go back in the timeline of a program, when a change causes issues or bugs.

### Classes taken

In IT-145 I learned about app development and how to handle several different kinds of input. I developed a simple but effective zoo monitoring application. This is the artifact I use to showcase my enhancements, and I cover it in the Refinement Plan.

In CS-250 I learned how the software development lifecycle functions and about the many roles in it. I looked at each role to learn the mindset of the product owner, project manager, testers, and coders, so I could understand each person's role and communicate with teammates.

In CS-310 I learned an important lesson about teamwork and collaboration, and it was my first time using integrated Bitbucket in an IDE. In this program, my classmates and I contributed side by side on a jukebox program. We added unit tests for each module we added, and we had to edit the main program with proper source control methods to include our new methods, data, and anything else our piece needed.

In CS-340 I learned how client/server development worked. We built many server and client programs. We also learned about creating and managing threads on a server and how they interact with a client application, which improved our knowledge of both sides of the development process, as well as the security involved when operating those relationships within a company.

These are not the only things I learned in the Computer Science program, but I believe they highlight important learning experiences and professional development from earning my degree. They are wide-ranging lessons that will help me, and others on any team I am on, learn together.

## To the Future

Currently, my senior DBA position is with the state, which is a good learning position in an advanced area of databases, in this case SQL Server. I hope to learn from this experience and either move into a managerial position or a more full-stack position at a private company, preferably with remote working options. My time in the military has given me an aptitude and the skills necessary for leadership. I was the highest individual in my field, which was radio repair. As the only person who knew radio, I also took care of routine maintenance, installation, and inventory management. I had to make reports and decisions for the entire company regarding the radios, report those to my company command, and take their feedback into a plan of action for how to achieve our goals. I had to locate people and train them to perform the tasks quickly, while making sure they knew exactly what needed to be done, so the work only needed to be checked later and not fixed when I made my rounds. The military taught me attention to detail, how to stick to a schedule, and how to work under pressure. I hope to turn these skills into professional experience in the civilian sector.
